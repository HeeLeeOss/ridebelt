# Preliminary Patentability & Freedom-to-Operate Review (2026-10-14)

> **Project decision (2026-10-14): Path A, fully open, no patent filings.** The patentability sections (§1, §4, §5) are kept as a historical record. The publication of this repository now serves as **defensive publication** of the RideBelt features. The **freedom-to-operate sections (§3 and §6) still apply**: being open source does not license anyone to third-party patents, so the design-arounds stay in force. See [business/licensing-model.md](../business/licensing-model.md).

> ⚠️ **This is not a legal opinion.** It is a first-pass web search (Google Patents and USPTO) to guide a conversation with a registered patent attorney. Legal status comes from Google Patents, which labels it an *assumption*. Claim readings are summaries. Many claims were truncated in the sources. **Get a professional prior-art search and a written freedom-to-operate (FTO) opinion before filing, disclosing or selling.**

## 1. Bottom line
| Question | Preliminary answer |
|---|---|
| Can we patent the **broad idea** ("a rider-worn backup restraint that clips to the ride")? | **Probably not.** There is close prior art: zip-line harnesses with secondary safety lanyards, Universal's rider-worn harness that clips into seat brackets, sky-coaster harnesses, and coaster seat belts linking the seat to the harness. |
| Are there **narrower inventions** worth filing? | **Yes, several** (§4). The best candidates are the Convertible Center Strap, the RAS anchor and gear-compliance check, and possibly the full system combination. **Round 2 (§6) changes this:** the Center Strap still stands. The compliance check must be narrowed to its ride-class and service-life logic, because Skyline's US 12,661,596 shows RFID-gated zipline launch. The RAS anchor's keyed release and release under load are known. |
| Does anything **block** us? | **One high-priority risk:** Universal City Studios' **US 11,975,679 B2** (active, to about 2042) covers rider-worn harnesses that clip into ride-seat brackets. The leg-loop-free Hip Belt appears to avoid its independent claims. **The Shoulder Module + Center Strap + RAS bracket combination needs attorney review.** Several other active patents affect only optional features, and can be designed around. **Round 2 (§6) confirms this:** the granted EP counterpart (EP 4 380 708 B1) also requires a vest plus two leg restraints. We found no US continuation, but the CN, JP, KR and CA members are pending and need watching. The TEAM IX family has fully lapsed. No new blocking patent found. |

## 4. What looks patentable (candidates for a provisional filing)
Ranked by estimated strength:

1. **Convertible Center Strap (strongest standalone candidate).** One strap fixed to the belt's front, with a **single free-end connector** that fits both a seat-pan center anchor and the belt's rear ring. Two **sewn length stops** (anchor or wearable length), with a mode indicator. The single connector makes the two modes **mutually exclusive by construction**. We found no reference with this seat-anchor ↔ wearable conversion. *Round 2: still none found.* Expect an obviousness rejection built on the tie-back snap (RE44,077: a hook clips back onto its own webbing), US 4,927,211 (a crotch strap linking harness and vehicle belt) and racing sub straps. Put the weight on the length stops, the mode indicator and the connector geometry (§6.3a).
2. **Rider-gear compliance check at a ride anchor (method and system).** An anchor or operator reader reads a cryptographic tag on rider-worn restraint gear and checks authenticity, service life, arrest-event retirement, and whether the modules **required for that ride class** are fitted, then gives a dispatch or interlock signal. LATCH sensing and ride RFID exist separately. This combination of gear compliance and ride-class rules appears new. Claim around the *compliance logic*, steering clear of wireless lock actuation (US 12,134,366). **Round 2 narrows this candidate.** RFID-gated dispatch is already known: Skyline's US 12,661,596 reads rider and equipment tags at a zipline launch and blocks release on a mismatch. RFID-tagged harnesses for inspection tracking (DBI-SALA i-Safe, 2006; Edelrid Smart Belay), restraint verification before dispatch (Disney US 12,522,174) and crypto tag authentication are known too. The independent claim must require reading at the **seat anchor**, plus a **service-life or arrest-retirement** check and a **ride-class required-module** check (§6.3b). Still a candidate, but now medium strength.
3. **RAS anchor assembly.** A secondary anchor on the seat structure, in a **load path independent of the primary restraint**, with geometry that stops cross-loading, an **operator-only mechanical release**, and a clipped-in indicator. Each element alone is probably known. The combination as a *secondary* rider-gear anchor may be claimable, but expect obviousness rejections. **Round 2 weakens this candidate.** Operator-only and key or tool release on ride buckles is old (US 5,665,002, DE 203 08 068 U1, US 7,166,032, US 9,776,095), and so are release under load (US 4,337,913) and latch-engaged sensors (US 6,220,171). Rely on the independent load path, the anti-cross-load geometry, and pairing with candidate 2.
4. **Full system claim.** A low hip belt without leg loops + a two-side rear tether geometry at a defined angle range + a center strap anchored to the seat, on a ride seat that also has a primary restraint. This is weaker (racing harnesses, Universal '679), but it is useful as a fallback dependent claim set.
5. **Design patents** on the hip-belt shape and the themed shells (cheap; protects appearance, not function). **Trademarks** for "RideBelt" and "RAS", and a certification mark for "RAS-certified".

**Not worth claiming:** the broad "rider-worn backup restraint" idea; the Universal Wrap Strap (slings and chokers are well known); the 3-point geometry alone.

## 5. Strategy recommendations
1. **Stop public disclosure until a provisional is filed.** The U.S. gives a one-year grace period, but most other countries require *absolute novelty*: any public disclosure before filing destroys foreign rights. This repo should stay private.
2. **File a U.S. provisional application** covering candidates 1–4 with the drawings and specs from `docs/design/` and `docs/standard/`. It is cheap, and it holds the priority date for 12 months.
3. **Commission a professional search and FTO opinion**, centered on US 11,975,679 (including continuations and foreign counterparts), US 12,134,366, and the gamification/tracking families.
4. **An open standard and patents can coexist.** Publish the RAS *interface* openly. Patent the anchor and compliance-check implementations, and license them cheaply under FRAND-style terms (the USB/ISOFIX model). Consider **defensive publication** of the minor features we won't patent, so nobody else can.
5. **Consider approaching Universal.** They own the closest active patent and clearly invest in harness-style ride restraints. A license, cross-license or partnership could turn the main blocker into the first RAS adopter.
6. **Search further** (not yet done): CPC A63G 31/16 (ride passenger restraints), A62B 35/00 (harnesses), B60R 22/12 (anti-submarine belts); Espacenet and WIPO for EP, DE (Mack, Gerstlauer, Intamin) and CN filings; legal status of the US 7,905,789 family and US 11,603,061.

### 6.4 Round-2 conclusions (summary of changes to §1, §3 and §4)
- **No new blocking patent found.** The Universal '679 reading is confirmed by the granted EP claims (vest + two leg restraints). Its pending CN, JP, KR and CA members and any unpublished US continuation are now **watch items**.
- **The TEAM IX family has fully lapsed.** US 11,603,061 belongs to Mack and covers a lap-bar lock, so it raises no RideBelt FTO issue.
- **New low-risk FTO items:** Skyline US 12,661,596, 3M US 12,201,858 / 12,654,044, Disney US 12,522,174, Universal US 12,559,054 and Mission Systems US 9,162,089.
- **Patentability:** candidate 1 (Convertible Center Strap) **still stands**, with obviousness risk noted. Candidate 2 is **narrowed** to the ride-class and service-life compliance logic at the anchor. Candidate 3 is **weakened**: its keyed release and release under load are each known.
