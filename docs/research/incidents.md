# Incident Review (Phase 0)

**Purpose:** find which ride types and failure modes RideBelt would actually have helped with, and which it would not.

> ⚠️ Status labels: **Verified (primary)** = checked against a regulator or official report; **Verified (secondary/press)** = checked against reputable press or trade coverage that reports official findings, but the underlying report was not read; **Unverified** = single or weak source. Anything not "Verified (primary)" must be re-checked against the primary document before it is cited externally. Victims are not named. Last web review: Oct 2026.

## Sources to mine
- U.S. CPSC (mobile rides) and NEISS injury data
- State ride-safety agencies (e.g., FL FDACS, PA Dept. of Agriculture, CA DOSH, TX TDI, OH Dept. of Agriculture, CO CDLE Oil & Public Safety, ME State Fire Marshal, WI DSPS, NY DOL)
- Non-US regulators: Technical Safety BC and TSSA (Canada), Japan MLIT (elevator and amusement ride accident committee), Sweden SHK, UK HSE
- SaferParks database and the Woodcock monthly ride-incident recaps (woodcock.blog.torontomu.ca)
- IAAPA and ASTM F24 incident summaries
- UK HSE reports, and EU national reports (Germany's TÜV and others)
- Court filings and wrongful-death suits, which often include engineering expert reports

**Reading the tally honestly:**
- This is a convenience sample. Ejection incidents were searched for deliberately, so it says nothing about how often ejections happen compared with other ride injuries (most reported injuries are not ejections).
- About 1 in 3 incidents is a plausible **Yes** (mostly R1 and R3). About 2 in 3 are **Yes or Partial**, but "Partial" usually means "only if the rider actually clipped in and someone checked". That is the same human check that failed in the R2 cases.
- All 6 S1/T1 incidents are **No**.

## Classification scheme
For each incident, record:
1. **Ride type:** inverting coaster, non-inverting coaster, drop tower, pendulum, swing, spinning or flat ride, water ride, or other
2. **Failure mode:**
   - R1 restraint mechanical failure (latch, hydraulics, structure)
   - R2 restraint not locked or not checked (operator or sensor)
   - R3 rider fit outside the restraint's design range
   - R4 rider action (unbuckling, standing)
   - S1 structural failure of the seat, car or arm
   - T1 trajectory or collision (no restraint involvement)
3. **RideBelt applicability:** Yes, Partial or No, with a short reason.

## Early takeaways (updated)
- RideBelt can help only with **R1–R4**. It does nothing for S1 or T1, and the marketing must not suggest it does. S1 shows up repeatedly in recent incidents (Fire Ball, Quimera, Jetline, Taif).
- **R3 is the strongest case.** Perilous Plunge, Joypolis, Klotten and Orlando FreeFall all involved bodies the ride's own restraint could not hold. Covering a wider anthropometric range than the ride's restraint remains the biggest potential contribution, so the size range RideBelt actually fits must be tested.
- **R2 is the most common restraint mode, and an optional belt doesn't fix it by itself.** The Haunted Mine Drop (sat on the belts) and Urayasu (interlock read "locked" with the bar raised) cases show that any unchecked item can be defeated. A **"clipped-in" signal read by ride controls** is what turns RideBelt from Partial into Yes (see the RAS draft).
- **Child riders** appear often (Drop Zone, Haunted Mine Drop, Palace Playland, Galaxy Spin). Small-body fit and child-resistant release need explicit design attention.
- **Water rides:** a tether in a raft or boat that can capsize may add drowning risk (cf. Splash Canyon). Treat water rides as out of scope unless analyzed separately.
